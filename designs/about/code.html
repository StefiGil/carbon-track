About CarbonTrack

A free web tool that estimates the carbon footprint of educational institutions from their monthly energy consumption.

info

Built for institutions in Argentina

The emission factors used here, especially the electricity factor, reflect Argentina's national power grid. Results are not valid for institutions in other countries.

What it does

CarbonTrack provides a standardized, rigorous calculation pipeline designed for students, teachers, and sustainability committees in educational institutions. It runs entirely in the browser with no installation required, offering an immediate diagnostic baseline that can be easily updated over time. In future iterations, this open methodology may extend to hospitals, municipalities, and commercial facilities.

How it works

1

Data input

The user enters or uploads monthly consumption logs (kWh, m³, liters) for their campus facilities.

2

Processing

Consumption is matched against verified emission factors per source and year, then systematically aggregated by source, month, and scope.

3

Results

Diagnostic metrics, visual distribution dashboards, and an official PDF summary ready for academic or institutional reports.

Emission factors and sources

Emission factors are verified and stored directly in the system database; users never manually enter or calibrate them. Because the national generation mix changes over time, electricity maintains a distinct, official factor for each historical year (from 2006 to 2023), ensuring accurate retrospective analyses.

Why are recent years sometimes not calculated?

The Secretaría de Energía publishes the official electricity grid factor with a delay of approximately two years. Consumption entries from years without a published factor are not calculated, and the final generated report explicitly notes which calendar years were excluded.

help_outline Why Simple Operating Margin (SOM)? expand_more

The Simple Operating Margin (SOM) reflects the carbon intensity of the power plants that actually ramp up or down to cover marginal demand in the Argentine Interconnection System (SADI).

Because additional electrical load is predominantly satisfied by fossil thermal generation, SOM provides a far more accurate representation of the real-world atmospheric consequence of campus energy consumption than average generation mix figures.
